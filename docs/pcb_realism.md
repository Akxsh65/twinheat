# PCB realism notes

Research notes from 2026-10-24. **Not requirements.** Nothing here changes a constant, a spec or
CLAUDE.md. Every sampler or spec change proposed below is a decision for its owner (CLAUDE.md §0
rule 2, §2). All sources are web pages found in one search pass; none is in CLAUDE.md §16, so none
may be cited in the report without the team's approval (§0 rule 10). Confidence labels: **High**
= several independent sources or a primary datasheet, **Medium** = one vendor source,
**UNVERIFIED** = remembered or not confirmed in the text retrieved.

## 1. What the generator is today

The board sampler (CLAUDE.md §6.5, §7.4) is a **statistical generator of thermally relevant
inputs**, not a PCB layout generator. It draws the fields the heat equation needs (copper
fraction per layer, via density, heat sources, convection, heat-sunk edges). It does not draw a
design that a fab could build or that passes electrical or manufacturing rules.

| Realistic already | Synthetic / simplified |
|---|---|
| 4-layer 1.6 mm stack, prepreg 0.20 / core 1.06 / prepreg 0.20 mm, copper weights 0.5–2 oz | Chips are plain rectangles with uniform power over the footprint (A4); no package, no exposed pad, no die size, no leads |
| Thermal via arrays under heat sources (p = 0.6), 0.3 mm drill, 25 µm plating | No netlist, no routing, no DRC, no courtyards, no electrical function |
| L2 ground plane with cut-outs, L3 split plane | "Traces" are 3.2–6 mm wide Manhattan strips (power or pour-like); real signal traces are far thinner and are only represented through the average copper fraction φ |
| Chip power 0.1–3 W, total ≤ 6 W, envelope θ_max ≤ 100 K | Copper shapes are placed independently of chips (random centres), so hot parts are not tied to pads or pours except through the via arrays |
| Chip gap ≥ 2 mm, edge margin ≥ 3 mm; heat-sunk edges; forced-air zone | Plane connections are modelled as ideal copper: no thermal-relief spokes, no antipad rings |

For the guarantees this is acceptable: Lemma 1 holds for any proposer and Lemma 2 for any
M-matrix, whatever the board looks like. Realism affects **external validity**: what "in
distribution" means, how the surrogate behaves on boards people design, and E17 (real boards).

## 3. Findings that matter for TwinHeat

1. **The 2512 resistors for E17 fall outside the sampler's chip sizes.** A 2512 is 6.3 × 3.2 mm,
   but `chip_short_side` is U[4.7, 20] mm. The 4.7 mm comes from "chips ≥ 3 cells" at N = 64
   (3 × 1.5625 mm). At N = 256 three cells are 1.17 mm and a 2512 is about 8 cells across, so the
   v2 grid can represent it, but §7.4 does not widen the sampler range. As written, the held-out
   hardware boards would be out of distribution for the surrogate.
2. **Inner copper weight.** CLAUDE.md §7.1 has 35 µm inner planes; the fab default found is
   17.5 µm (35 µm is an option). Halving the plane copper halves its sheet conductance
   (390 × 17.5e-6 = 6.8e-3 W/K versus 1.4e-2 W/K). §7.1 already says to use the as-fabricated
   stack; the G1.2 fab check is where this gets settled.
3. **Via density range matches practice.** Real thermal arrays (pitch 1.0 to 1.2 mm) are 0.7 to
   1.0 via/mm², the top of the sampled range U[0.2, 1.0]; `ood_dense_vias` (> 1 via/mm²) is a
   pitch below 1 mm. Sensible.
4. **Real heat sources are tied to copper.** Every exposed-pad or tab part sits on a copper land,
   usually connected to a pour; the sampler places copper independently. The surrogate may see
   too few "hot part on a pad" cases and too many "hot part on bare FR-4" cases. The vias under
   chips (p = 0.6) only partly compensate.
5. **Heat enters through the pad, not the package body.** For QFN and PowerPAD parts the heat
   footprint is the exposed pad (smaller than the body); for leaded parts it enters through the
   leads. A4 deposits power under the footprint, which is a fair board-scale approximation only
   when the footprint is the heat path. This stays a model-form term in the H10 budget.
6. **Not modelled** (model-form items for the E17 uncertainty budget): thermal-relief spokes,
   antipad rings around signal vias, solder mask, component height, radiation (A5), edge
   convection (A6), temperature-dependent properties (A3).

## 4. Proposals (each needs its owner's decision; none is applied)

| ID | Proposal | Owner | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | For v2 chips, allow short side down to about 3 cells at N = 256 (1.17 mm), or add a 2512-sized archetype, so E17 boards are in distribution | M (sampler), with D2 | Changes the sampler distribution; must be done before data generation (wk 3 to 4) |
| R2 | Add a copper land (φ = 1) under each chip footprint, at least the exposed-pad size, and tie it to the via array | M | Changes G under chips; interacts with A4 |
| R3 | Replace "uniform random rectangle" by a small set of **component archetypes** (QFN/PowerPAD regulator, DPAK/D2PAK or SOT-223 on a copper tab, SOIC, BGA-class processor, 2512 resistor), each with body size, pad size, power range and via-array rule taken from datasheets | M | Needs datasheet numbers I did not retrieve (package body sizes are **UNVERIFIED** here); richer but more work |
| R4 | Correlate copper with chips: grow a pour from each hot pad with some probability | M | Moderate; keeps §6.5 shapes |
| R5 | Fix the inner copper weight to the chosen fab's stack in `config/spec_v2.yaml` once `docs/refs/fab_stackup.txt` exists | N | Trivial |
| R6 | Add the unmodelled items in section 3, point 6 to the H10 uncertainty budget | G | None |

R1 and R5 look necessary for E17 to be meaningful; R2 to R4 improve external validity but cost
sampler time. Whether a full layout-style generator is worth it for a 12-week project is the
team's call; the guarantees do not need it.
